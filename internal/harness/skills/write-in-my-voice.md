---
description: Learn how the user writes from their sent mail and save it, so every draft sounds like them.
---
1. Check memory_search "writing style" first. When a style is saved and the user did not ask to relearn it, use it and stop.
2. Read 15 to 20 recent emails the user wrote themselves with gmail_search "in:sent newer_than:60d" and gmail_get_message. Skip forwards, one-line replies and automated mail. Include mail to colleagues and to people outside their organisation.
3. Note what is consistent:
   - Greeting and sign-off, and whether they change with the recipient.
   - Length and structure: short lines, paragraphs or bullet points.
   - Tone: formal or casual, direct or softened, use of exclamation marks and emoji.
   - Habits: words and phrases they use often, British or American spelling, how they say no or ask for something.
4. Save the style with memory_save as one fact that starts "Writing style:" and is under 800 characters, for example: "Writing style: greets with 'Hi <first name>,' and signs '- Ebot'; 2 to 4 short lines; direct, no exclamation marks; British spelling; says 'Thanks' not 'Thank you'."
5. Show the user the saved style with one sample reply written in it, and ask them to correct anything that is off.
6. Never save the contents of anyone's emails, only how the user writes.
